## Claim

A wagering interface should let the player set three numbers the house already knows, in the open, before the skill game runs:

1. **Bet amount** (what the player risks)
2. **Win amount** (what the player takes if the resolution wins)
3. **Skill factor** (how much a measured score is allowed to move the probability of that resolution)

Call that triple the BetCube. The house still sets legal min/max, house edge, and the hold. The player chooses a point inside that box. The machine shows the implied chance of winning before play, then updates that chance after a score if skill factor is not zero.

The related math model (publicly called NanoTech Advantage) compares the player's score to a score database, weighted by bet amount and skill factor. Above-average scores may receive a higher chance of winning. Below-average scores receive a lower chance. The operator's long-run hold stays at or above the configured house edge because advantage given to skilled play is taken from weaker play, not printed from nothing.

Skill play and random resolution are separate stages. A high score does not by itself pay the win. It can only change the odds of the later resolution, and only by as much as skill factor and law allow. Skill factor of zero is a pure chance bet.

## Interface, said plainly

Before the ball is in play the player can see a wheel or equivalent: green is win probability for this bet/win pair at the current skill setting, red is the rest. Changing bet or win resizes the sectors. Changing skill factor changes how much a later score is allowed to resize them.

After the skill game, score is compared to the database. If skill factor is above zero, the wheel is redrawn. Then the resolution runs.

That is the whole product idea. Transparency of expected value and chance-to-win is the point. Hidden RTP sliders are the thing it was built against.
